# Training Notes

## Leadership Baseline

Management focuses on How: SOP, standardization, efficiency, method, and control.

Leadership focuses on Why: change, challenge, innovation, mission, values, trust, respect, and meaning.

Use both. A failing team often has too much management and too little leadership.

## System Thinking

Team management is a complex system. Individual optimum may hurt the whole.

External-view checklist:

1. Essence: what is the root problem?
2. Tradition: has this appeared before, and what worked or failed?
3. Boss: how does the superior see it?
4. Peers: how do parallel teams see it?
5. Subordinates: what impact does it have on team members?
6. Customers: what do important customers need?
7. KPI: where do personal, boss, and company KPIs align or conflict?

## Responsibility

Avoid external attribution. For problems, inspect:

- what I did not clarify
- what I did not follow up
- what risk I did not surface
- what resource I did not request well
- what commitment I can now make

Good upward reporting contains facts, plan, owner, timeline, resource request, risk control, and result commitment.

## Talent Enablement

Use ASK:

- Attitude: selection, role model stories, value assessment, recognition.
- Skill: rotation, authorization, skill contests, mentor pairing, OJT.
- Knowledge: exams, weekly sharing, case analysis, skill maps.

OJT sequence:

- I say, you listen.
- I do, you watch.
- You do, I watch.
- You say, I listen.

## Goals and Execution

MBO means goals and standards are agreed in advance, then the employee receives enough autonomy.

Project action plan fields:

- key task
- owner
- collaborator
- checker
- start and end time
- output document
- communication mechanism
- needed resource
- risk and countermeasure
- result and reward or consequence

Use 415:

- 40% effort before the meeting
- 10% in the meeting
- 50% after the meeting through follow-up and closure

## Retrospective

Use before, during, and after action reflection.

Fact restoration:

- what happened
- what I did
- what others did
- what result appeared

Cause analysis:

- what I thought then
- why I thought so
- what others may have thought
- what assumptions were hidden

Emotion separation:

- what emotion did I have
- how did it affect judgment
- what would I judge without that emotion
- can I now stay rational

Task review:

- original goal
- current result
- variance
- success factors
- failure factors
- keep
- improve
- start
- stop

## Collaboration

Cross-team collaboration is active selling, not passive coordination.

First do everything possible to reduce the other side's burden. Use common leader escalation last.

Stakeholder checklist:

- decision maker, contact, related people
- benefit and damage to the other team
- compensation for damage
- meaning, recognition, emotional value
- difficulty, doubt, complexity
- multiple options for choice
- KPI intersection
- team load and timing
- trusted third party
- shared credit

## Feedback

Positive feedback:

- define topic
- describe facts, details, data, behavior, and feeling
- invite the person to explain causes
- connect impact to team value
- connect motivation to values and growth

Negative feedback:

- define topic
- describe facts and data
- describe short and long term impact
- analyze causes together
- agree improvement plan and support

## Motivation

People differ in psychological needs. Check:

- recognition
- autonomy
- learning and growth
- participation
- tools and support
- career path
- relationship atmosphere
- work-life pressure
- security

For D/I/S/C tendencies:

- D often values result, autonomy, challenge, and fast decision.
- I often values recognition, atmosphere, visibility, and relationship.
- S often values stability, trust, support, and low-conflict transition.
- C often values quality, evidence, standard, and predictability.

Use this only as a hypothesis. Observed behavior wins.

DISC misuse risks:

- D: escalation can happen too early. Check stakeholder preparation, decision rights, and tradeoff before escalating.
- I: factual feedback can still damage trust if public feedback caused loss of face. Include relationship repair, recognition, and belonging.
- S: a mandated change may produce silent resistance. Use safety, small step transition, support, and predictable milestone.
- C: quality concern may be mistaken for obstruction. Define done, risk tier, evidence, decision record, and release tradeoff.

## Delegation

Five-point delegation:

- starting point: background and meaning
- ending point: SMART result and assessment
- nodes: milestones
- blockers: hard points and countermeasures
- focus: big picture and key decisions

Delegation is talent development. The manager must tolerate temporary imperfection while controlling real risk.

## Engagement

Use Q12-style checks:

- does the person know what is expected?
- do they have materials and support?
- can they use strengths?
- were they recognized recently?
- does someone care about them?
- is someone encouraging development?
- is their opinion valued?
- does the mission make work important?
- are peers committed to quality?
- do they have trust relationships?
- has progress been discussed?
- did they have learning and growth chances?

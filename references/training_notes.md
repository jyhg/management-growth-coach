# Training Notes

## Leadership Baseline

Management focuses on How: SOP, standardization, efficiency, method, and control.

Leadership focuses on Why: change, challenge, innovation, mission, values, trust, respect, and meaning.

Use both. A failing team often has too much management and too little leadership.

## Personal Conduct And Responsibility

Leaders are highly visible in a room. Their behavior is amplified, so small signals can affect trust, pressure, and team norms.

When taking responsibility, use this response pattern:

- state the current result and impact
- own the part you did not clarify, follow up, or escalate in time
- give countermeasures and resource needs
- commit to the next result and time point

Do not use explanations as the main output. Use them only after the plan is clear.

## System Thinking

Team management is a complex system. Individual optimum may hurt the whole.

External-view checklist:

1. Essence: what is the root problem?
2. Tradition: has this appeared before, and what worked or failed?
3. Boss: how does the superior see it?
4. Peers: how do parallel teams see it?
5. Subordinates: what impact does it have on team members?
6. Customers: what do important customers need?
7. KPI: where do personal, boss, and company KPIs align or conflict?

## Responsibility

Avoid external attribution. For problems, inspect:

- what I did not clarify
- what I did not follow up
- what risk I did not surface
- what resource I did not request well
- what commitment I can now make

Good upward reporting contains facts, plan, owner, timeline, resource request, risk control, and result commitment.

## Proactivity

Circle of influence vs circle of concern:

- Circle of influence: things the manager can directly act on or gradually expand.
- Circle of concern: things the manager should understand but cannot directly control.

Spend most energy on the circle of influence. Convert complaints into controllable next actions.

Internal vs external attribution:

- External attribution tries to change others first.
- Internal attribution asks what I can adjust to influence others.

Use internal attribution first, then decide what support or escalation is needed.

## Talent Enablement

Use ASK:

- Attitude: selection, role model stories, value assessment, recognition.
- Skill: rotation, authorization, skill contests, mentor pairing, OJT.
- Knowledge: exams, weekly sharing, case analysis, skill maps.

OJT sequence:

- I say, you listen.
- I do, you watch.
- You do, I watch.
- You say, I listen.

IDP personal development plan:

- current performance and strengths
- possible career direction or succession direction
- development gaps
- target action
- completion time
- needed resources
- development method
- expected result
- manager suggestion

Talent enablement reminders:

- willingness to develop matters
- capability can often be developed, but values usually need selection
- beware the curse of knowledge when teaching
- combine training with real work using the 70-20-10 idea
- allocate coaching energy based on task type, talent density, risk, willingness, and team load

## Goals and Execution

MBO means goals and standards are agreed in advance, then the employee receives enough autonomy.

Project action plan fields:

- key task
- owner
- collaborator
- checker
- start and end time
- output document
- communication mechanism
- needed resource
- risk and countermeasure
- result and reward or consequence

Use 415:

- 40% effort before the meeting
- 10% in the meeting
- 50% after the meeting through follow-up and closure

3+1 performance goal model:

- key performance outputs: quantity, speed, quality, cost, or other measurable result
- work tasks: decomposed tasks, temporary priorities, project process tasks
- improvement and innovation: cost reduction, efficiency, suggestions, sharing
- personal capability development

Task decomposition methods:

- step method: decompose by process steps
- element method: decompose by key factors
- formula method: identify variables in a result formula
- brainstorming: use after basic research, then add, reduce, combine, or prioritize

Execution inspection tools:

- green-cross calendar for visible execution tracking
- task closure rate table
- rotating inspection mechanism

Inspection should be frequent and transparent. The goal is to inspect work and help people, not to surprise or punish.

## Retrospective

Use before, during, and after action reflection.

Reflection timing:

- before action: premortem and failure rehearsal
- during action: adjust while executing
- after action: review facts, causes, and lessons

Fact restoration:

- what happened
- what I did
- what others did
- what result appeared

Cause analysis:

- what I thought then
- why I thought so
- what others may have thought
- what assumptions were hidden

Emotion separation:

- what emotion did I have
- how did it affect judgment
- what would I judge without that emotion
- can I now stay rational

Task review:

- original goal
- current result
- variance
- success factors
- failure factors
- keep
- improve
- start
- stop

10-10-10 decision reflection:

- What happens after 10 seconds?
- What happens after 10 months?
- What happens after 10 years?

Blue-team challenge:

- assign someone to challenge assumptions
- keep conflict inside the meeting room
- after decision, align and execute together

## Collaboration

Cross-team collaboration is active selling, not passive coordination.

First do everything possible to reduce the other side's burden. Use common leader escalation last.

Stakeholder checklist:

- decision maker, contact, related people
- benefit and damage to the other team
- compensation for damage
- meaning, recognition, emotional value
- difficulty, doubt, complexity
- multiple options for choice
- KPI intersection
- team load and timing
- trusted third party
- shared credit

Johari window for trust building:

- open area: known to self and others
- blind area: unknown to self, known to others
- hidden area: known to self, unknown to others
- unknown potential area: unknown to both

Expand the open area through appropriate self-disclosure, feedback, and shared work.

New employee six key moments:

- offer acceptance
- first day
- first week
- first month
- first mistake
- first excellent performance

## Feedback

Positive feedback:

- define topic
- describe facts, details, data, behavior, and feeling
- invite the person to explain causes
- connect impact to team value
- connect motivation to values and growth

Negative feedback:

- define topic
- describe facts and data
- describe short and long term impact
- analyze causes together
- agree improvement plan and support

Feedback timing:

- praise should not wait until tomorrow
- criticism should not wait overnight when impact is fresh and facts are clear

## Motivation

People differ in psychological needs. Check:

- recognition
- autonomy
- learning and growth
- participation
- tools and support
- career path
- relationship atmosphere
- work-life pressure
- security

Psychological needs ranking can include:

- being valued and appreciated
- tolerance for innovation and risk
- advanced tools and technical support
- transparent communication
- competitive rewards
- interesting work
- career development
- management style fit
- participation
- learning opportunities
- personal care
- security
- flexible environment
- work-life balance
- interpersonal atmosphere

Motivation tools:

- vision: describe development possibility and meaning
- wow moment: create unexpected positive moments
- hygiene vs motivator distinction: remove dissatisfaction, then create satisfaction
- non-authority influence: suggest rather than only command
- emotional account: trust must be deposited before hard feedback

Veteran employee motivation:

- urgency: exposure to strong peers, challenging projects, or performance standards
- respect: mentoring roles, rituals, visible contribution
- freshness: new projects, rotation, renewed challenge

New employee activation:

- ownership: participation in goal setting and meaningful authorization
- emotional value: growth, fun, challenge, and timely feedback
- circle identity: peer network, reverse mentoring, safe expression

Q12-style engagement survey:

- clear expectations
- needed materials and support
- chance to use strengths
- recent recognition
- manager or colleague care
- encouragement for development
- opinions valued
- mission makes work important
- peers committed to quality
- trusted work relationship
- progress discussion
- learning and growth opportunity

For D/I/S/C tendencies:

- D often values result, autonomy, challenge, and fast decision.
- I often values recognition, atmosphere, visibility, and relationship.
- S often values stability, trust, support, and low-conflict transition.
- C often values quality, evidence, standard, and predictability.

Use this only as a hypothesis. Observed behavior wins.

DISC misuse risks:

- D: escalation can happen too early. Check stakeholder preparation, decision rights, and tradeoff before escalating.
- I: factual feedback can still damage trust if public feedback caused loss of face. Include relationship repair, recognition, and belonging.
- S: a mandated change may produce silent resistance. Use safety, small step transition, support, and predictable milestone.
- C: quality concern may be mistaken for obstruction. Define done, risk tier, evidence, decision record, and release tradeoff.

## Delegation

Five-point delegation:

- starting point: background and meaning
- ending point: SMART result and assessment
- nodes: milestones
- blockers: hard points and countermeasures
- focus: big picture and key decisions

Delegation is talent development. The manager must tolerate temporary imperfection while controlling real risk.

Game achievement incentive:

- challenging goals
- frequent feedback
- personalized and multidimensional recognition
- team competition or ranking
- some uncertainty or surprise

Team-building design dimensions:

- vulnerability: real stories and appropriate openness
- emotion: moments that create care and connection
- laughter: relaxed shared activities
- physical energy: sports or active participation
- taste and belonging: informal, high-frequency, voluntary groups
